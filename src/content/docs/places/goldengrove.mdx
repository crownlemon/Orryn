**Goldengrove**, also known as **Castle Goldengrove**, is the ancestral seat of [House Rowan](/houses/rowan/) in the northern Reach. It lies east of Red Lake, amid the rich farmlands and orchards of the Northmarch. The castle stands upon a hill above the Mander, overlooking the river meadows and the roads that lead through Rowan country.

## Description

### Castle and grounds

Goldengrove is built of **pale stone**, its walls, towers and crenellations taking on a golden colour in the late evening sun. The castle rises above the river, approached by a road that follows the water beneath willow trees before climbing the hillside between dry-stone walls. The golden tree of House Rowan flies above the gate.

Its great hall is long and high, with a gilded tree carved into the oak wall behind the dais. The high table stands beneath the carved branches, while the household knights dine below. A smaller room in a tower above the river serves as Lord Rowan's private chamber, reached by a narrow stair.

The castle also has stables, a tilting yard, guest chambers and a paved courtyard. A river wall provides a cooler place for horses during the summer heat. Below the castle lie **orchards and river meadows**. Apples are an important feature of the surrounding land: young grafts are watered in dry weather, and carts carry water barrels up among the trees. Orchards also extend into the neighbouring countryside, where watered rows mark the approach to Goldengrove.

## History

Goldengrove was brought into the ancient Kingdom of the Reach through the pacts of friendship and mutual defence made by King Garth III Gardener. Its lords were among the great northern houses whose lands and military strength contributed to the power of the Gardener kings.

During **Aegon's Conquest**, the armies of King Mern IX Gardener of the Reach and King Loren I Lannister of the Rock assembled at Goldengrove before marching against Aegon Targaryen. Their united host was defeated at the Field of Fire.

During the **Dance of the Dragons**, Goldengrove surrendered to the green army led by Lord Ormund Hightower, its defenders fearing the dragon Tessarion of Prince Daeron Targaryen. Lord Thaddeus Rowan, who had fought for the blacks, died on the road home to Goldengrove in 135 AC. The castle was among the intended destinations of the royal progress planned for King Aegon III in 136 AC, before the journey was cancelled.

In the years following the Dance, the hereditary Marshalship of the Northmarch passed from House Osgrey to the Rowans, adding to Goldengrove's standing among the northern Reach lordships.

## Events in 208 AC

In 208 AC, Goldengrove was the residence of Lord Rycherd Rowan, his wife Lady Clarice Hightower and their children Ser Triston and Lady Roslin Rowan. The young **Lord Harys Oakheart** was being raised there under Rowan guardianship. Ser Alyn Merryweather also served among the household knights.

The betrothal of Ser Triston to **Lady Joy Penrose** brought Joy and her brother Robin to Goldengrove. Joy was welcomed as the future lady of the castle and shown its rooms, while the Penrose party remained among Rowan's guests.

That summer, **Lord Alaric Meadows** of Grassfield travelled to Goldengrove to speak with Rycherd concerning Lord Harys Oakheart and a proposed marriage to Alaric's daughter Elyce. The visit included supper in the great hall and a private conversation in the tower chamber above the river. The following day, Lord Rycherd and Alaric negotiated the purchase and transport of hay and oats for Grassfield, with Ser Triston attending to the loading and measurements.
