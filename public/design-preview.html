House Rowan

The golden tree of Goldengrove

House Rowan of Goldengrove is one of the oldest and most powerful noble houses of the Reach. Its lands lie along the region's northern approaches, and its lords hold Goldengrove in fealty to House Tyrell of Highgarden. The family's arms depict a golden tree on silver, recalling the legendary beginnings of its line. In the 208 AC chronology, the house is headed by Lord Rycherd Rowan, hereditary Marshal of the Northmarch.

Although bound to Highgarden, the Rowans possess lands, dependants, marriage ties and an authority of their own. Goldengrove has long been a centre of power among the northern Reach lords. The position of its reigning lord in the politics of Highgarden is therefore important, but should not be mistaken for submission to any one of Lord Leo Tyrell's sons.

History

Like several of the great Reach families, the Rowans trace their legendary ancestry to Garth Greenhand, through Rowan Gold-Tree. Their lineage is ancient, although the tales of its beginning belong to the realm of legend rather than a surviving contemporary record.

At Aegon's Conquest, Goldengrove served as a gathering place for the forces of King Mern IX Gardener of the Reach and King Loren I Lannister of the Rock. Men of House Rowan marched with the combined host defeated by Aegon Targaryen at the Field of Fire. During the later Faith Militant uprising, Lord Rickard Rowan and Lord Torgen Oakheart accompanied the followers of Septon Moon and protected them outside Oldtown. The family also took part in the civil war known as the Dance of the Dragons: Lord Thaddeus Rowan declared for the blacks and fought at the Honeywine, later serving as a regent and Hand during the reign of Aegon III.

The dignity of Marshal of the Northmarch passed from House Osgrey to House Rowan around 135–145 AC. By Lord Rycherd’s day, the marshalship was hereditary.

Events in 208 AC

Lord Rycherd Rowan maintains close relations with Ser Moryn Tyrell, whose political influence rests in part upon his ties to the older landed houses. Rowan's association with Moryn does not make Goldengrove a dependent factional household. The Rowans have ties of their own to Oldtown, the Stormlands and other powerful Reach families, and Lord Rycherd pursues his family's interests independently.

Goldengrove's marriage alliances have become especially significant. Lady Clarice, Lord Rycherd's wife, was born a Hightower and is a granddaughter of Prince Daemon Targaryen through Princess Rhaena. Her sister Lady Alysanne married into House Penrose. During 208 AC, letters sealing the betrothal of Ser Triston Rowan, heir to Goldengrove, and Lady Joy Penrose were exchanged before Chapter 60. The betrothal strengthened ties between the two families and built upon their existing royal kinship.

At the same time, Lord Harys Oakheart is in Lord Rowan's keeping at Goldengrove. His wardship has become relevant to discussions about a potential marriage involving Lady Elyce Meadows. The Rowan household also includes knights whose obligations extend beyond family blood, among them Ser Wendell Webber and Ser Alyn Merryweather.

Relations with Highgarden have also been complicated by the dispute at Cress Hill. Highgarden's Justiciar heard a quarrel between two of Lord Rowan's vassals, and Ser Tom Costen drew a knife during the proceedings. Rycherd asked for Costen to be sent to Goldengrove for judgment, regarding the case as a question of his own jurisdiction over Rowan vassals.

People of the Reach

Lord Rycherd Rowan

Lord of Goldengrove

Lord Rycherd Rowan is the Lord of Goldengrove, head of House Rowan and hereditary Marshal of the Northmarch in 208 AC. A powerful nobleman of the northern Reach, he is an experienced lord and an accomplished bargainer, attentive to customary rights and particularly sensitive to affronts to his family's standing.

Rycherd is close to Ser Moryn Tyrell but retains independent political and dynastic interests. He is not a Tyrell court officer by Moryn's appointment. His dignity as marshal belongs to his own house and predates the current rivalry among the Tyrell sons.

Character

Rycherd is proud, demanding and knowledgeable about the obligations of landed lordship. He understands patronage and bargaining, expects the lesser houses to acknowledge their place and maintains friendships and reciprocal duties among his neighbours. He seldom forgets a slight. Matters that another lord might regard as a temporary disagreement can become, in his judgment, questions touching the honour and authority of Goldengrove.

He regards himself as a guardian of the established feudal order. Yet his reluctance to compromise openly and his resentment of outside interference may strain the very relationships upon which that order depends. His conduct does not always reduce to stubbornness: an insistence on proper jurisdiction, sworn obligation and visible respect can also serve recognisable political ends. In the household, his reputation is that of a demanding lord who expects precedence to be observed.

Family

Rycherd married Lady Clarice Hightower around 180 AC. Clarice is the daughter of Garmund Hightower and Princess Rhaena Targaryen, giving the Rowan children descent from Prince Daemon Targaryen. Her younger sister Lady Alysanne married Ser Roland Penrose of Parchments, establishing a close familial link between the two houses. Rycherd and Clarice's known surviving children are Ser Triston (23), their heir, and Lady Roslin (17);

In 208 AC, Ser Triston was betrothed to Lady Joy Penrose, daughter of Lord Ronnel Penrose and Princess Elaena Targaryen. The letters were sealed shortly before Chapter 60, and the betrothal became public at Lady Leonette Meadows's wedding feast. The wedding itself remains a future event. Rycherd's younger brother Ser Nyles Rowan also belongs to the family;

History

Rycherd rules an old northern Reach lordship whose customary authority is reinforced by the hereditary marshalship of the Northmarch. This title passed from House Osgrey to the Rowans following the Dance of the Dragons following the Dance of the Dragons, long before Rycherd's present dealings with Ser Moryn Tyrell. His early life and the details of his accession are not yet established .

In the political life of Highgarden, Rycherd is counted among the great landed lords with whom Moryn cultivates close relations. That association is not an exclusive allegiance. His family maintains independent contacts with House Hightower and the Stormlands, while negotiations over the futures of his children provide opportunities to extend Goldengrove's influence without surrendering its autonomy.

The marriage of his heir to Lady Joy Penrose, arranged by betrothal in 208 AC, is one such undertaking. Other responsibilities are less voluntary. The young Lord Harys Oakheart is held in Rycherd's keeping at Goldengrove, a fact that has drawn the lord into discussions about the boy's possible future marriage. The presence of a noble ward carries both an obligation of care and political consequences for any proposed arrangement.

The Cress Hill affair has tested Rycherd's relationship with Highgarden more directly. When Highgarden's Justiciar heard a dispute involving two Rowan vassals, Ser Tom Costen drew steel in the hall. His conduct raised a question of punishment, but also of whose authority should ultimately be exercised over the knight. Rycherd's grievance concerns the jurisdiction of his house as much as the individual fate of Costen. Rycherd sought Costen’s return to face judgment at Goldengrove.
